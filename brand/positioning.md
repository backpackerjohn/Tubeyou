## 1. Category and positioning statement

**Category:** faceless long-form documentary — travel × history, forbidden/restricted/sealed
places [DECISION.md].

**Positioning statement:**

> **The inverted travel guide for the world's sealed places.** Every episode is a dossier —
> access status, the named law, the penalty on record, the last documented entry — and every
> episode ends with the one legal way in.

Rivals end at "you can't go." We end at the aperture [DECISION.md M2; skeptic-reports.md
C14 §(a)4]. The citation floor is not a disclaimer buried in the description — it is the
on-screen visual identity (the Entry File card, M1) and the thumbnail grammar
(dossier/permit/stamp, M2).

## 2. Audience: who watches, and the job the content does

**Who (grounded in what actually ranks and converts on our validated keywords):**

- **Geography/curiosity viewers** — the audience already watching RealLifeLore (9.67M views
  on "islands you are not allowed to visit"), Joe Scott (872K), and hochelaga (669K on the
  Vatican Archive keyword) [launch-keyword-validation.md K9, K10]. That these channels hold
  the head of our exact queries is direct evidence the searcher base is geography-explainer
  and curiosity-documentary viewers (inference from ranking overlap).
- **Long-form documentary watchers** — 27–44 min videos win on our keywords: The Explorer's
  Diary 663K on a 40m36s Mount Athos doc; Forbidden Mysteries 26K on a 44m31s doc at 23.7K
  subs [launch-keyword-validation.md K3, K10]; Hidden Planet Docs runs 31–44 min faceless
  docs to ~570K views/mo [finalist-validation.md].
- **Discovery traffic, not subscribers** — the reference class earns 1.3–5.1x its sub count
  in recent views via browse/search [taste-signals.md T4]. We plan for cold-traffic viewers
  who have never heard of the channel; every video must work as a first contact (inference
  from T4).

**The three jobs the content does:**

1. **Curiosity resolution** — Engine A titles open a stakes-laden gap ("banned,"
   "forbidden," penalty) and the dossier closes it with the actual record
   [taste-signals.md T3; DECISION.md].
2. **Background-watch** — 20–40 min segmented narration that survives with the screen off;
   the format class all six taste references share [taste-signals.md T1, C5; the screen-off
   test is also the anti-slideshow policy defense, skeptic-reports.md C14 §(b)].
3. **Trust** — the viewer learns that on this channel, the sensational claim always has a
   source on screen. Trust is the retention asset the countdown farms never build
   (inference; mechanism per taste-signals.md T8 and skeptic-reports.md C14 §(c): 5/5
   sampled topics anchor to named statutes, UNSC resolutions, UNESCO listings, prosecuted
   cases).

## 3. The promise

> **Every claim on screen has a paper trail. Every closed door has its legal aperture shown.**

Operationally: no place enters the roster without a citable legal/official basis (M4); every
segment opens on the Entry File — ACCESS STATUS / LEGAL BASIS named on screen / PENALTY ON
RECORD / LAST DOCUMENTED ENTRY (M1); every video closes on the documented legal way in —
permit, pilgrimage protocol, guided zone, research application (M2); legends get graded
against the record in a myth-audit beat, never repeated as fact (M4) [DECISION.md;
skeptic-reports.md C14 §(a)4]. This is one promise doing three jobs: audience trust,
Guardrail-6 differentiation, and the YPP author-input defense [DECISION.md M1].

## 5. Positioning risks, answered by M1–M4

**R1. "Validated by the thing we must differ from."** Hidden Planet Docs is simultaneously
our revenue proof and our clone risk — the same datapoint [skeptic-reports.md C14 §(a)2].
The wedge as originally pitched lived below the click layer, in a lane where the title
carries the whole click decision [taste-signals.md T3]. **Answer: M1 + M2** make the wedge
visible product from video 1 — the Entry File card on screen and the dossier/permit/stamp
thumbnail grammar, plus the "one legal way in" as a packaging element, not a footnote.

**R2. Inauthentic-content pattern-match.** One countdown template at scale + synthetic
narration + slideshow-class assets is YouTube's canonical rejection combination, in a lane
classifiers plausibly already see as polluted [skeptic-reports.md C14 §(b); ypp-policy.md
§2]. **Answer: M3** (three structurally distinct episode types in the first 10 videos —
Deep File / countdown / access-calendar special) plus **M1** as demonstrable per-segment
author input; M9 extends this with named-persona narration and per-video variation.

**R3. Fringe-CTR pressure.** The genre's highest-CTR claims are the undocumentable ones
("Google blurs it," hauntings), and a documented-only filter costs some crowd-pleasers
[skeptic-reports.md C14 §(c) caveat i]. **Answer: M4** — the myth-audit beat monetizes the
legend *as content* (address the lore, show the record) instead of repeating it or losing
the click entirely; positioning against slop is the ownable stance no probe result exhibits
[skeptic-reports.md C14 §(a)4].

**R4. Pool burn.** The documentable pool is ~100–130 places and countdowns consume ~10 per
video; uncapped, each place recycles ~6x/year and the catalog self-cannibalizes
[skeptic-reports.md C14 §(d)]. **Answer: M3** — episode rotation with a place-reuse cap;
Deep Files (1 place/video, 8 of 10 launch videos [launch-keyword-validation.md slate])
carry the long tail, and **M4**'s documented-access news stream (Varosha 2020, Okinoshima
2018 arcs) mints status-change specials without spending new places.

**R5. Positioning must never promise revenue it can't cite.** The honest month-6 band is
$450–1,500/mo, $2K crossing months 8–14 [DECISION.md]. The positioning above sells trust to
viewers, not projections to ourselves; the M6 tripwire (no video >10K views after 8–10
uploads → overhaul or kill) is the enforcement mechanism behind the brand claim that
packaging, not participation, is the variable [DECISION.md M6; finalist-validation.md].
