### 1.2 Decision — recorded

**WINNER: THE ENTRY FILES** (handle target: `@TheEntryFiles` — **verify at launch**, Tier B caveat above).

**RUNNER-UP: The No-Go Atlas** (falls back only if the @TheEntryFiles handle family is taken AND
no acceptable variant exists).

Reasoning, criterion by criterion:

1. **Dossier identity (the decisive criterion).** DECISION.md M1 makes the "Entry File" on-screen
   dossier card the mandatory visual identity of every segment. Naming the channel after the
   device makes brand = product: every video *is* an Entry File, every thumbnail stamp, every
   lower-third, every playlist ("File No. 014") extends the name instead of competing with it.
   The C14 policy skeptic's core demand — "ship the differentiation wedge as product, not prose,
   visible in packaging" (skeptic-reports.md, C14 required modification 1) — is satisfied *by the
   name itself*. No other candidate does this.
2. **Memorability.** Two common words, concrete image (a file being opened), double meaning
   ("entry" = getting in + a file entry). Says the format (files/dossiers) and the subject
   (entry/access) in two words.
3. **Title-machine fit** — `[Place]: [Hook] | The Entry Files` and the native episode form
   `ENTRY FILE No. 07 — Diego Garcia`. Short enough (15 chars) to survive title truncation as a
   suffix; also works as a prefix brand phrase, the Pitch-Meeting/OverSimplified brand-template
   class (taste-signals §3a).
4. **Trademark/collision risk.** The only candidate field entry with *both* no observed collision
   and no crowded namespace. N1/N3/N4/N5/N6 all live inside the Atlas namespace occupied by Atlas
   Obscura (places content, large brand) and near-homophone "The New Atlas" — avoidable risk we
   don't need to take, and differentiation-by-name from atlas-style incumbents is itself a
   Guardrail-6 asset (skeptic-reports.md C14 §a).
5. **Why the working name lost.** "The No-Go Atlas" is memorable and travel-coded, but it names
   the *category* (an atlas of no-go places — exactly what Hidden Planet Docs, Top Fives, and
   Paint Explainer's series already are, per finalist-validation.md) rather than the *wedge*
   (the citation-anchored dossier). The skeptic's clone charge is answered better by a name from
   the wedge side. It remains the runner-up because it scores highest of the rest on memorability
   and title fit, and its collision result was clean at the exact-match level.

**Naming conventions that follow:** episodes are "Files" ("Deep File", "Entry File No. N"),
countdowns are "Registries", status-change specials are "Status Updates", the narrator is
"the Registrar" (§2), the archive of covered places is "the Registry".

## 2. BRAND VOICE

### 2.1 Narrator persona: **The Registrar**

Per ypp-policy.md §3.5 and §4, a consistent **named narrator persona** is the channel's authorship
signal (no face required). Ours:

- **Name:** The Registrar. Credited in every description: *"Researched, written, and narrated by
  The Registrar."* It is openly a persona, never presented as a fake human biography — no
  invented backstory, no fake credentials (that would be its own authenticity problem).
- **Character:** a records clerk for the world's closed doors. Has read the statute. Has the
  case number. Mildly amused by legends, never sneering. Files everything; exaggerates nothing.
- **Register:** measured, declarative, precise. Reads like a court record narrated by someone
  who finds court records genuinely thrilling. Dry wit is allowed one beat per segment, always
  at the expense of a myth, never of a victim or a people.
- **Signature verbal devices (use every episode):**
  - Opens segments by "pulling the file": *"File forty-one. Varosha, Cyprus. Access status: sealed."*
  - Cites on speech: *"Security Council Resolution five-five-oh, nineteen eighty-four. Paragraph five."* (matching on-screen citation, M1)
  - The myth-audit pivot: *"That's the legend. Here's the record."* (M4)
  - The closing beat, every video, verbatim family: *"Every door has one legal way in. Here's this one."* (M2)
- **Consistency rule:** same voice (human VO, or the same human-directed premium voice per §7),
  same pacing band, same credit line, from video 1 (ypp-policy §4: persona from launch).

### 2.2 Tone rules

1. **Authoritative and specific.** Prefer the named instrument to the vague category: "the
   Andaman & Nicobar (Protection of Aboriginal Tribes) Regulation, 1956" — never "local laws"
   (source class proven available for 5/5 sampled topics, skeptic-reports.md C14 §c).
2. **Zero fringe claims.** Nothing airs that cannot anchor to a statute, official designation,
   court case, or reputable dated reporting (M4). Legends are covered *as legends*, then graded
   against the record (the myth-audit beat). This is anti-Atlas-Adam positioning by design
   (taste-signals W3).
3. **Penalties are cases, not threats.** "You could be arrested" is banned; "In 2018, the men who
   ferried John Allen Chau were arrested" is the house style (M1: PENALTY ON RECORD means an
   actual prosecuted case).
4. **Respect for the excluded and the expelled.** Indigenous-protection zones, expulsions
   (Chagossians), and sacred restrictions are treated as history with stakes, not spooky content.
   The forbidden-ness is the hook; the people are never the punchline.
5. **End on the aperture.** Every video closes with the documented legal way in (M2). We are the
   inverted travel guide: rivals end at "you can't go"; we end at the door.
6. **Uncertainty is stated, on the record.** If a number is an estimate, say "estimated"; if a
   claim is single-sourced, say so. Precision about uncertainty *is* the brand.

### 2.3 Banned words and moves (hard rules, per taste-signals W3/W7)

**Banned title/thumbnail framings (soft-framing flops — W7's documented examples: "sealed for
1,000 years" 2,928 views vs 18,423 channel avg; "Libraries Destroyed" 1,087 vs 8,405):**
- Titles without a stakes/prohibition clause ("The History of Niʻihau" — dead on arrival).
- Passive mystery-mush: "mysterious", "strange", "eerie", "creepy", "unexplained", "secrets of"
  as the load-bearing hook word. ("Secret" is allowed only when literally official: "secret
  cities" is the actual ZATO descriptor.)
- Hedged claims: "may", "might", "could possibly", "some say" in titles.

**Banned fake-mystery moves (W3 — fringe without anchor):**
- "Scientists can't explain…" / "They don't want you to know…" / "Google blurs it" (a documented
  false claim — skeptic-reports.md C14 §a lists it as exactly the anti-example).
- Presenting hauntings, curses, or aliens as live possibilities. They may appear only inside a
  myth-audit segment, explicitly graded against the record.
- Un-cited numbers. Every figure in a title or on a card traces to an archived source (M4). If
  the count is contested (e.g. closed-city totals: launch slate says 41 per
  launch-keyword-validation.md K8; skeptic-reports.md §d says ~38 documented) the number is
  **verified during scripting before it may appear in a title**.
- Implying the viewer can sneak in, or providing evasion instructions (also a policy/CG risk).
- Clickbait inversion: never promise access we don't show; the "one legal way in" is always real
  and always cited.

## 3. TITLE SYSTEM

### 3.1 Engine

All titles run **Engine A: curiosity gap + stakes** (taste-signals T3) with a **citation-anchored
twist clause** — the differentiation the skeptic demanded at the click layer. Never Engine B,
never mixed-weak (W7).

**Skeleton S1 — Deep File (single place):**
`[The] {place descriptor} + {prohibition/consequence clause} — {legal/record twist}`
The twist clause after the em-dash is our signature: it points at the *record* (a law, a date, a
person, a number), which no farmed rival title does (skeptic-reports.md C14 §a4).

**Skeleton S2 — Countdown/Registry:**
`{N} {place category} You {Can't/Aren't Allowed to} {Visit/Enter} ({record-twist parenthetical})`

**Skeleton S3 — Status Update (access-calendar special):**
`{Place} Just {Reopened/Closed/Changed Its Rules} — {what the document says}`

**Brand phrase placement:** YouTube prepends the channel name everywhere titles are shown, so the
suffix `| The Entry Files` is **not** included in the title field (wasted characters). The brand
phrase lives in the thumbnail stamp, the spoken open, and the episode numbering ("Entry File
No. 07") in descriptions and pinned comments.

### 3.3 Countdown-number rules (S2 variants)

1. **The number is a claim.** Use a verified count when the record supplies one (the ZATO list, "3
   sealed caves") — a documented, non-round number is a credibility signal and a differentiator.
   When curation sets the number, default to **10** (the proven magnitude across Ancient Indy,
   Atlas Adam, and the K9 probe results — taste-signals T2, launch-keyword-validation.md K9).
2. **Place-reuse cap (M3/M4 depth protection):** no place appears in more than one countdown per
   rolling 6 months, and never as a countdown entry within 3 months of its own Deep File. Track in
   the Registry ledger (skeptic-reports.md C14 required modification 4: the ~100–130-place pool
   burns at ~10 places/countdown).
3. **Countdowns are capped at ~1 in 3 uploads**; the rotation Deep File / countdown / status
   special is a build requirement (M3), not a preference.
4. Every countdown title carries the record-twist parenthetical — that parenthetical is the brand
   at the click layer (e.g. "(And the One Legal Way Into Each)").

### 3.4 A/B variant protocol (implements DECISION.md M6)

1. **Before upload:** every video has 2 pre-written titles (primary + V variant, one leading with
   the curiosity clause, one leading with the place name) and 3 thumbnail variants entered into
   YouTube's Test & Compare (thumbnail A/B) feature.
2. **First 72 hours:** no changes; let Test & Compare run on thumbnails.
3. **Day 4–7:** if the video's CTR-driven impressions clearly trail the channel's rolling median
   for its slot, swap to the V title (one swap only; log both titles + outcome in the Registry
   ledger). Never swap title and thumbnail simultaneously — one variable at a time.
4. **Month-2 tripwire (M6, binding):** after 8–10 uploads, if **no video has passed 10,000
   views**, stop production and run a full packaging overhaul (title skeletons + thumbnail
   grammar re-derived from what the ledger shows) — or kill per DECISION.md. The ledger of A/B
   outcomes is the overhaul's raw material; keeping it is mandatory from video 1.
5. Floor-case reminder taped to the desk: same niche, weak packaging = ~2,100 mean views
   (Untouched Mysteries, finalist-validation.md). Packaging is the variable.

## 4. THUMBNAIL GRAMMAR — the dossier/permit/stamp system (M1/M2)

One glance must say: *classified travel dossier, opened for you*. All 10 launch thumbnails must
read as one channel in a subscriptions grid.

### 4.1 Fixed layout (every thumbnail)

Canvas 1280×720. Three layers, always in this order:

1. **The place (60–75% of frame):** one high-contrast photographic or satellite image of the
   place, color-graded to the house grade (slightly desaturated, lifted blacks, warm paper cast —
   so stock photos from different sources match). No collages, no more than one place image.
2. **The file furniture (the brand layer):**
   - A **manila folder tab** clipped to one edge (default: top-left), carrying the file number in
     typewriter type: `FILE No. 03`. Same corner every video — grid consistency.
   - EITHER a **paper strip** along the bottom (15–20% height) with the place name in condensed
     caps, OR a **torn-paper edge** on one side — never both.
3. **The stamp (the hook layer):** ONE rubber stamp, rotated 6–10°, ink-distressed, oversized
   (25–35% of frame width), placed over the image but never over faces or the geographic focal
   point. Allowed stamp texts (pick the one that matches the video's hook):
   - `ACCESS DENIED` / `NO ENTRY` / `SEALED` — red (§4.3)
   - `PERMIT ONLY` / `RESTRICTED` — red
   - `ONE LEGAL WAY IN` — **green** (the M2 aperture, our ownable stamp; use on countdowns and
     any video whose hook is the aperture)
   - `REOPENED {YEAR}` / `STATUS CHANGED` — green (status specials)

### 4.2 Text rules

- **≤ 4 words of overlay text** beyond the stamp and place name. The title does the talking (T3).
- Stamp type: stencil caps (Saira Stencil One or Allerta Stencil — free Google Fonts).
- Place name / labels: condensed grotesk caps (Barlow Condensed SemiBold or Oswald).
- File-furniture details (file numbers, dates): typewriter (Special Elite or Courier Prime).
- Never more than these three type families. No drop shadows on type; use the paper strip or a
  10% ink scrim for contrast.
- Numbers in countdown thumbnails: the numeral huge (≥ 40% frame height), stencil, ink-black or
  stamp-red on manila — the numeral *is* the stamp on countdown thumbnails.

### 4.3 Color palette (hex, binding)

| Role | Name | Hex |
|---|---|---|
| Folder/manila field | Manila | `#E7D9B4` |
| Paper highlight | Old Paper | `#F4EDDC` |
| Ink / text / redaction bars | Registry Ink | `#16130E` |
| Prohibition stamps, alert accents | Stamp Red | `#B3271E` |
| "One legal way in" / reopened stamps | Permit Green | `#1F6E43` |
| Map lines, satellite accents, links | Chart Blue | `#2E5A6B` |
| Tape, worn edges, secondary labels | Khaki | `#9C8B6B` |

Rules: Stamp Red and Permit Green never appear in the same thumbnail (the stamp states ONE
verdict). Backgrounds are the photo or Manila — never pure white or pure black fields. Red is
reserved for stamps/alerts only (never body text), so it keeps its charge.

### 4.4 Do / don't (described examples)

**DO — File No. 01 (North Sentinel):** full-bleed aerial of the island ringed by surf, house
grade; manila tab top-left `FILE No. 01`; bottom paper strip `NORTH SENTINEL ISLAND` in Barlow
Condensed ink-black; red `NO ENTRY` stamp at 8° across the upper-right sea, clear of the island
itself. Nothing else.

**DO — File No. 02 (flagship countdown):** manila folder background, one island photo "clipped"
inside it at a slight angle, giant stencil `10` in Stamp Red filling the left half, green
`ONE LEGAL WAY IN` stamp lower-right. Reads as: a folder of ten files, and a way in.

**DON'T:** red arrows, yellow circles, shocked-face insets, "GONE WRONG"-style flame text —
generic-slop grammar; breaks the dossier system and pattern-matches the farmed lane
(skeptic-reports.md C14 §b). No AI-fantasy renders of real places presented as photos (disclosure
+ credibility problem, §7). No more than one stamp; a thumbnail with two stamps is shouting. No
haunted-green grading or horror fog (fringe-coded, W3). Never place the stamp dead-center flat at
0° — it must read as hand-stamped, not templated.

**Consistency check before publish:** put the new thumbnail in a 3×4 grid with the previous nine.
If tab position, type families, grade, or stamp language differ from the system — fix before
upload. (This grid check is the M9 "one channel, varied episodes" balance: layouts S1/S2/S3 vary
by episode type; the furniture, palette, and type never vary.)

### 5.3 Lower-third "Entry File" dossier card (M1 — mandatory in every segment)

The on-screen card that opens every place segment. It is the citation floor made visible and the
author-input evidence for YPP review (DECISION.md M1).

- **Form:** an index-card panel (Old Paper, 1px Registry Ink rule border, subtle paper texture)
  sliding in from the left, occupying the lower third; a small red wax-less stamp mark
  `ENTRY FILE No. {NN}` on its tab.
- **Fields, always these four, always this order** (labels in Barlow Condensed caps, Khaki;
  values in Special Elite, Registry Ink):
  1. `ACCESS STATUS:` — controlled vocabulary: BANNED / PERMIT ONLY / SEALED / FROZEN /
     INVITATION ONLY / PARTIALLY REOPENED {year}
  2. `LEGAL BASIS:` — the named instrument, dated (e.g. "A&N (PAT) Regulation, 1956" ·
     "UNSC Res. 550 (1984)")
  3. `PENALTY ON RECORD:` — a real prosecuted/enforced case, dated (e.g. "2018 — 7 arrested
     after Chau landing")
  4. `LAST DOCUMENTED ENTRY:` — named person/party, date, outcome
- Values that are estimates carry `(est.)`; unknown is written `NO RECORD FOUND` — never left
  blank (the honesty is the brand).
- Animation: typewriter-fill of values over ~2.5s while the Registrar reads them; a red or green
  status stamp thunks onto the card last. Same rig every episode (build once in the editor as a
  motion-graphics template).
- The card doubles as the segment chapter marker in the timeline and as the frame we crop
  community-post images from — one rig, three uses.

### 5.4 End screen (last 20s)

Registry Ink desk; two manila folders side by side (element slots: next video left, launch
playlist right), each with a typewriter label tab (`NEXT FILE` / `THE FIRST TEN FILES`); subscribe
element styled as a red stamp reading `FILE UNDER: SUBSCRIBED` bottom-center. The Registrar's
closing line plays over it: the "one legal way in" beat resolves here, so the end screen is
calm — no music sting louder than the voice. Same layout every video (end screens are furniture,

## 7. AI-DISCLOSURE STANCE (per ypp-policy.md §2–4)

### 7.1 The stance, in one line

**Disclose whenever the "altered content" definition is met; never rely on synthetic media so
realistic that the disclosure is doing load-bearing work.** Disclosure is monetization-safe;
concealment is what gets punished (ypp-policy.md §2: the label doesn't reduce reach; failing to
disclose risks demonetization/strikes).

### 7.2 Checkbox rules (decision table at upload)

| Situation | "Altered content" checkbox |
|---|---|
| Realistic synthetic/cloned voice presented as a real narrator | **YES** — and per §7.3 we avoid this configuration anyway |
| Photoreal AI imagery depicting a real place/event (e.g. a generated "photo" of Poveglia's ward) | **YES** — and banned from thumbnails/passed-as-real use per §4.4; if ever used in-video it is stylized, captioned `RECONSTRUCTION`, and disclosed |
| Clearly stylized maps, diagrams, the Entry File card rig, chart animations | No (explicitly out of scope: "clearly unrealistic/animated content") |
| AI used for research assistance, script drafting aid, editing, upscaling, color | No (out of scope: scripting/brainstorming/editing assistance) |
| Licensed archival footage, own-shot assets, CC photos, Google Earth-class satellite imagery | No |

When genuinely borderline: check the box. One consistent over-disclosure costs nothing; one
missed required disclosure risks the channel (ypp-policy §2). But do not disclosure-spam clearly
stylized content — that dilutes the label's meaning (ypp-policy §4 checklist).

### 7.3 Narration approach

- **Preferred: human VO by the operator as The Registrar.** No disclosure required; strongest
  authenticity signal at YPP review.
- **Fallback: premium human-directed TTS** (DECISION.md M9 allows it). If the voice is realistic
  enough to be mistaken for a human speaker, check "Altered content" on every such upload and
  carry a standing description line: *"Narration voice is synthetic, directed by The Registrar;
  the script is human-researched and human-written."* Flat robotic TTS is banned outright — it is
  the canonical inauthentic-content rejection example (ypp-policy §2).
- **Provenance file per video (M1/M9 defense):** retain script drafts with edit history, the
  source archive (fetched primary documents), and the asset manifest. This is the evidence pack
  if the channel ever faces inauthentic-content review (ypp-policy §4: keep drafts as provenance).
- **Never:** verbatim AI-generated scripts read to slideshow visuals (the exact demonetization
  pattern, ypp-policy §2); voice-cloning any real person; synthetic "witness" audio.

### 7.4 Standing pre-launch verifications (carried from DECISION.md M8 / skeptic mod 5)

Before first upload: re-verify YPP thresholds and inauthentic-content wording against the
official help pages from an unblocked network (ypp-policy.md §4 last item); verify Google Earth
Studio commercial terms; run the CC-license audit workflow on all launch-thumbnail photography;
verify the `@TheEntryFiles` handle and run the trademark knock-out search (§1.1).
