## C01 — The Ledger Breaks (economic-collapse forensics)

**Genres:** history
**One-liner:** Forensic financial autopsies of empires, nations, and companies that ran out of money — history told through the balance sheet instead of the battlefield.
**Format:** Long-form narrated documentary, 25-40 min, chaptered (The Boom / The Hidden Liability / The Run / The Reckoning), 1-2/week.
**Hook engine:** A-curiosity-stakes

**Title template:** How {Entity} Went Broke: {shocking mechanism or consequence clause} — e.g. "How {Empire/Nation/Company} Went Broke: {It Defaulted N Times / They Executed the Banker / The Money Died in {N} Days}". Variables: 60+ documented sovereign defaults (Reinhart-Rogoff catalog), 20+ hyperinflations, 15+ company/bank collapses = 95+ enumerable titles.

**Best sample titles:**
1. How Spain Went Broke 4 Times and Kept the World's Gold
2. France Executed Its Own Banker — Then Went Bankrupt Anyway
3. The Company That Owned India — and Still Went Bankrupt
4. Money Died in 15 Days: The Worst Hyperinflation in History (Not Weimar)
5. The Loan That Killed the Knights Templar

**Ownable reframe:** Retell famous historical collapses as forensic financial autopsies in which the balance sheet — not the battle — is the murder weapon, with the actual numbers reconstructed on screen.

**Production model:** Solo, ~10-14 hrs/video. Original script from academic sources (Reinhart-Rogoff, EH.net, central-bank histories); visuals are public-domain paintings/engravings, coin/banknote photos, self-built animated debt/inflation charts (original assets, zero licensing risk), simple animated maps. Human-directed premium TTS or own VO with named narrator persona; every claim cited on-screen. No third-party footage, so reused-content exposure is near zero.

**Why now / demand:** rpm-history.md (Tier B, snippet-level) explicitly says history blended with economics/finance lands at the TOP of the $4-9 RPM range because it pulls finance-adjacent advertisers — the highest-RPM wedge inside the genre, directly serving the $2,000/mo goal at lower view counts. Demand signal (Tier B): Economics Explained grew to millions of subs on macro-collapse explainers, and a dedicated "Economy Historian" channel already occupies an adjacent lane — evidence of appetite; its existence is a competition item for the downstream audit, not proof of saturation.

**Key risks:**
- Economics Explained / Economy Historian adjacency — must verify downstream that sub-500k channels rank for collapse keywords (kill criterion 1)
- Chart-heavy format risks lower retention than story-first rivals; scripting must keep narrative ahead of data
- RPM evidence base is confidence-LOW snippet triangulation — revenue model needs re-verification
- Same title skeleton at scale must carry per-video structural variation to avoid the "templated at scale" inauthentic-content pattern

## C02 — Margin of Failure (how-it-was-built engineering thrillers)

**One-liner:** Pre-modern megastructures retold as engineering thrillers: every video is the story of the collapse that should have happened — and the trick that prevented it (or didn't).
**Format:** Long-form narrated documentary, 20-35 min, chaptered by failure mode (The Problem / What Everyone Else Did Wrong / The Trick / The Test), 1-2/week.
**Hook engine:** A-curiosity-stakes

**Title template:** "{Structure} Should Have Collapsed — {withheld-explanation clause}" plus sibling skeleton "How {Builders} Built {Structure} Without {modern technology}". Variables: 100+ candidate structures x failure-mode angles; documented real collapses (Beauvais, Fidenae, Tay Bridge) supply the stakes proof.

**Best sample titles:**
1. The Pantheon Should Have Collapsed 1,900 Years Ago — Here's the Trick Holding It Up
2. How Brunelleschi Built the Impossible Dome Without Scaffolding
3. Hagia Sophia Survived 20 Major Earthquakes. Its Architects Died Thinking It Wouldn't.
4. Rome's Aqueducts Should Have Failed Every Winter. They Didn't.
5. How Medieval Masons Built 40-Meter Stone Ceilings With No Math for It

**Ownable reframe:** Turn commodity architecture history into engineering thrillers where the protagonist is the failure mode the builders had to outwit — an autopsy of a disaster that usually didn't happen, mechanism explained on screen.

**Production model:** Solo, ~12-15 hrs/video. Public-domain survey drawings/blueprints (HABS/HAER), period engravings, self-made 2D cutaway/force-diagram animations (the mechanism explainer IS the transformation layer), licensed stock establishing shots, optional AI reconstructions with "Altered content" disclosure. Diagram animation is the cadence bottleneck; a reusable cutaway-template rig keeps it to 1-2/week.

**Why now / demand:** Evergreen curriculum-adjacent fascination (taste-signals T5) with a stakes reframe no reference channel owns — differentiates from Ancient Indy's "forbidden places" engine. Tier B demand: multiple "Ancient Engineering" series are licensed across Tubi/Prime/CuriosityStream — broadcasters keep buying this topic — and "How Did Ancient Humans Build Everything?" style videos surface as popular YouTube results. Mid-range history RPM per rpm-history.md; engineering skews male 25-54, the premium demo cited there.

- "Ancient mysteries" adjacency — thumbnails/titles must stay mechanism-first or it drifts into the saturated clone space
- Physics claims need engineering-literature citations; errors get called out hard (also the credibility moat)
- Streaming incumbents upload full episodes free — must verify small channels rank on these keywords downstream

## C04 — Paper Trail (citation-anchored declassified-document mysteries)

**Format:** Long-form narrated documentary, 20-30 min, structured as a document read (The File / What It Admits / What's Still Redacted / What Happened Next), 1-2/week.
**Hook engine:** A-curiosity-stakes

**Title template:** "{Agency} Kept This File Secret for {N} Years — {withheld payoff clause}" with sibling skeleton "Declassified: The {document type} That {revelation clause}". Variables: CIA CREST (millions of pages), FBI Vault, NSA archive, NARA, UK National Archives; 100+ named operations x per-document angles.

**Best sample titles:**
1. The CIA Kept This File Secret for 42 Years — It Admits the Coup
2. Declassified: The Memo Where the Pentagon Planned to Fake an Attack on America
3. This Page Was Redacted for 50 Years. Here's What Was Under the Ink.
4. Declassified: The $20 Million Cat the CIA Actually Built
5. The War Game That Nearly Started a Real One: The Able Archer File

**Ownable reframe:** Make the primary-source document itself the on-screen protagonist — every sensational claim is shown in the government's own words at the moment it's made, fusing Ancient Indy's forbidden-knowledge hook with a built-in citation floor no mystery channel matches.

**Production model:** Solo, ~10-12 hrs/video. US government documents are public domain (CIA CREST/FOIA reading room, FBI Vault, NARA) — free, unlimited, legally clean core assets. Visual language: slow scan pans, highlight/redaction-reveal motion graphics (reusable rig), public-domain photos and film, period maps; own VO or human-directed premium TTS. Original analytical script synthesizing the file with published scholarship keeps it outside slideshow/inauthentic-content territory; the citation is the footage.

**Why now / demand:** Evergreen conspiracy-adjacent curiosity with a defensible wedge: the saturated mystery space runs on unsourced claims (Atlas Adam's flagged weakness, W3), while this format makes the Nature-paper credibility pattern the entire visual identity. Tier B: multi-hour "CIA Declassified" marathons and full-doc channels (ENDEVR, Forgotten History) surface as popular results, showing sustained binge demand; recurring FOIA releases (JFK files etc.) provide predictable spike-riding moments.

**Key risks:**

- Crowded "CIA secrets" keyword space — the document-on-screen wedge must be visible in thumbnails; verify small-channel rankability downstream
- Reading documents on screen must never become the whole video (slideshow pattern) — analysis layer mandatory every upload
- Conspiracy-adjacent audience pulls toward fringe claims; drifting past what documents actually say destroys the premise (W3)
