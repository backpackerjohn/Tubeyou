## 1. YPP eligibility thresholds (as of 2026-07-19)

Two-tier system, consistent across all sources checked:

| Tier | Subscribers | Long-form path | Shorts path | Other gates | Unlocks |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Tier 1 — Expanded YPP / fan funding ("early access")** | 500 | 3,000 valid public watch hours in last 12 months | 3,000,000 valid public Shorts views in last 90 days | 3 public uploads in last 90 days | Super Thanks/Chat/Stickers, Channel Memberships, Shopping |
| **Tier 2 — Full monetization (ad + Premium revenue)** | 1,000 | 4,000 valid public watch hours in last 12 months | 10,000,000 valid public Shorts views in last 90 days | — | Watch-page + Shorts-feed ad revenue share, Premium revenue |

Applies to both tiers (per YouTube Help pages 72851 / 13429240, corroborated by vidIQ,
TubeBuddy, SubSub, Shopify):

- Follow YouTube channel monetization policies (Community Guidelines, ToS, copyright, AdSense
  program policies).
- Live in a country/region where YPP is available.
- **No active Community Guidelines strikes.**
- 2-Step Verification enabled on the Google account.
- One active, linked AdSense for YouTube account.
- Watch time generated by Shorts-feed views does **not** count toward the 4,000 long-form
  watch hours — the two paths are separate.

Faceless-channel note: **there is no "face on camera" requirement anywhere in YPP
eligibility.** Facelessness itself is never a rejection reason; the content policies below are.

## 2. "Inauthentic content" / reused content policy

### The July 15, 2025 update

- YouTube renamed its **"repetitious content"** policy to **"inauthentic content"** effective
  July 15, 2025, describing it as "a minor update" to "better identify mass-produced and
  repetitious content." YouTube stressed such content "has always been ineligible for
  monetization" — the YPP has "always required 'original' and 'authentic'" content
  (YouTube Help 1311392; Social Media Today; TubeBuddy; iMusician).
- YouTube (via creator liaison Rene Ritchie) clarified this was **not** an "AI ban": the change
  targets "content that is repetitive or mass-produced," i.e. "AI slop," not AI-assisted
  production per se (Social Media Today).

### What "inauthentic content" means

Content that (per YouTube's published description, corroborated across sources):

> "follows a template with minimal variation, is easy to reproduce at scale, and lacks clear
> author input or meaningful substance."

Examples repeatedly cited as demonetization triggers for faceless channels:

- Robotic/synthetic narration reading text the creator didn't write.
- Image slideshows or scrolling text with minimal narrative, commentary, or educational value.
- Near-identical templated videos published at scale ("mass-produced").
- Compilations/clips with nothing added.

### Reused content policy — unchanged

YouTube explicitly said the **reused content** policy did not change in July 2025:

> "You can still post content from other platforms, or re-post videos on YouTube, and it will
> remain eligible for monetization if you've added significant original commentary,
> modifications, or educational or entertainment value to the original video."
> — YouTube, July 2025 (via Social Media Today / TubeBuddy)

Commentary, reaction, clips, and compilation formats stay monetizable **if transformed**.

### AI content rules and disclosure

- YouTube's stated position (July 2025): "YouTube welcomes creators using AI tools to enhance
  storytelling, and channels that use AI in their content remain eligible for monetization"
  (Social Media Today; YouTube even supplies AI tools — auto-dubbing, Dream Screen).
- **Disclosure requirement** (rolled out from March 2024, blog.youtube "Disclosing AI-generated
  content"; enforced through 2026): creators must check the **"Altered content"** box at upload
  when content is *meaningfully altered or synthetically generated and could be mistaken for
  real* — realistic synthetic people/voices (deepfakes), fake depictions of real events/places.
  YouTube then applies a "Altered or synthetic content" label.
- **Not** requiring disclosure: AI used for scripting/brainstorming, editing assistance,
  clearly unrealistic/animated content, color/lighting effects, beauty filters.
- The label itself does **not** reduce reach or monetization; **failing to disclose** when
  required is what risks label-forced application, reduced reach, demonetization, or strikes
  (blog.youtube; multiple 2026 secondary sources).
- **[LOW]** Several SEO blogs claim a January 2026 escalation: a wave terminating 16 large
  AI-slop channels (~4.7B views), "mandatory disclosure for all synthetic media," and a
  three-strike YPP suspension ladder. These specifics appear **only** in low-reliability
  sources (lastplaydistro, ytzolo, outlierkit) and could not be verified against official
  pages this session. Treat enforcement-is-tightening as directionally true, the specific
  numbers/mechanisms as unverified.

## 3. What keeps faceless/AI-assisted channels monetized (documented practices)

Consistent across YouTube's own statements and every secondary source checked:

1. **Original scripts** — write (or heavily author) your own scripts; never narrate scraped or
   AI-dumped text verbatim.
2. **Substantial transformation of any third-party footage** — visible editing, framing,
   analysis; the video must be pointless without your added layer.
3. **Real commentary/education/entertainment value** — research, storytelling, a distinct
   analytical angle, "human insight."
4. **Per-video variation** — avoid one rigid template reproduced at scale; vary structure,
   visuals, pacing across uploads.
5. **A recognizable authorial voice/persona** — consistent host identity (can be a named
   narrator or avatar) so reviewers see "there is a real creator here."
6. **Human-in-the-loop production** — AI as tool (drafting, b-roll, TTS polish, dubbing), with
   human curation, fact-checking, and editorial judgment on every video.
7. **Honest disclosure** — check "Altered content" whenever realistic synthetic media is used;
   disclosure is monetization-safe, concealment is not.
8. High-quality synthetic narration is acceptable when paired with 1–3; flat robotic TTS over
   slideshows is the canonical rejection example.

## 4. Design implications for our channel — checklist

- [ ] Plan for **Tier 2 from day one**: 1,000 subs + 4,000 long-form watch hours (12 mo) OR 10M
      Shorts views (90 days); long-form watch hours are the realistic path for an essay channel.
- [ ] Keep 3+ public uploads / 90 days from launch (also satisfies Tier 1 early access at 500
      subs + 3,000 hours for memberships/Super Thanks revenue before full YPP).
- [ ] Enable 2FA, set up AdSense, pick a YPP-available country of record before applying.
- [ ] Zero tolerance for Community Guidelines strikes during the ramp (active strike = ineligible).
- [ ] Every video gets an **original, human-authored/edited script** — no verbatim AI dumps, no
      scraped text; keep script drafts as provenance evidence for YPP review.
- [ ] Bake in a **signature format with per-video variation** — deliberate structural/visual
      differences between uploads so the channel never pattern-matches "templated at scale."
- [ ] Any third-party or stock footage must be **subordinate to our narration/analysis** —
      significant commentary, editing, and educational value on top (reused-content safe harbor).
- [ ] Build a **consistent narrator persona** (name, voice, tone) as the channel's authorship
      signal, even with no face on camera.
- [ ] Use a natural, produced voice track (human VO or premium TTS with human direction) — never
      flat robotic reading; narration quality is an explicit review signal.
- [ ] **Disclose via "Altered content"** on any video with realistic synthetic elements
      (cloned/synthetic voice presented as real, photoreal AI scenes of real events); don't
      disclose-spam on clearly stylized content where it's not required.
- [ ] Cap automation: human review/curation pass on every video before publish
      (fact-check, edit, thumbnail, metadata) — no fully hands-off pipeline.
- [ ] Publish cadence favors quality over volume; mass-production velocity is itself a policy
      signal against us.
- [ ] Re-verify all thresholds and the inauthentic-content wording against
      support.google.com/youtube/answer/72851 and /answer/1311392 from an unblocked network
      before launch (this session could not fetch official pages).
