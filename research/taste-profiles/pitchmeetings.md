## Format & Hook

- Two-character sketch comedy: a screenwriter "pitches" a real movie/show to a producer, exposing its plot holes and lazy writing through escalating dialogue. Single performer plays both roles.
- Rigid franchise title template: **"[IP] Pitch Meeting"** — 24 of 25 recent titles follow it exactly. The brand phrase carries the promise; the IP carries the search/browse demand.
- Topic engine: timed to releases and streaming-cycle nostalgia (Supergirl, Mandalorian & Grogu, Avatar: Fire and Ash within release windows; Die Hard/Home Alone at Christmas; A New Hope, Street Fighter 1994 as catalog nostalgia).
- Signature catchphrases ("super easy, barely an inch", "so the movie can happen") function as in-group identity (inference from format familiarity; not API data).

## Why the Angle Works (inference, grounded in fetched stats)

1. **Criticism-as-comedy transformation.** The raw material (a movie's plot) is commodity; the original value is 100% in the scripted dialogue — a defensible transformation model under reused-content rules even though it discusses others' IP.
2. **IP demand surfing with an evergreen wrapper.** Every release cycle mints new topics (Supergirl 1.27M views 2.5 weeks after upload), while old IP remains viable (A New Hope 490K, Street Fighter '94 594K) — topic supply is effectively infinite.
3. **Franchise-formula titling** builds a clickable brand promise identical in function to OverSimplified's "[Event] - OverSimplified": the format is the hook, the topic is the variable.
4. **Catalog recycling:** compilations convert existing episodes into 16–28 min watch-time units at near-zero marginal production cost — a monetization-efficiency pattern directly reusable by faceless channels.
