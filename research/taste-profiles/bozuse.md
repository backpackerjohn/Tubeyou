1. **It serves purchase-intent search/browse demand.** "Upcoming games 2026" is a query people run repeatedly; the 5.1x views-to-subs ratio shows the traffic comes from non-subscribers discovering these videos, so a small channel (33.7K subs) can pull 100K–780K views per video.
2. **The template is infinitely repeatable.** Same skeleton (N games x category x timeframe), rotated across genres (RPG, horror, FPS, co-op, survival, open world, cyberpunk, single-player) and time windows (month, season, year, event). 457 videos in under 3 years proves the engine sustains.
3. **Curation + stakes-based packaging is the value-add.** The underlying footage is publishers' trailers; the differentiation is selection ("Nobody Is Talking About", "That Aren't Call Of Duty") plus emotional framing ("Will Ruin Your Friendships"). The best performers pair a broad category with a strong claim.
4. **Event coattails.** Uploads timed to Summer Game Fest / State of Play (June 2026 cluster: 4 uploads in 3 weeks) ride predictable hype spikes.
5. **The risk is visible in the data.** The 200x view spread (3.8K to 780K) shows the format lives or dies on topic selection and title/thumbnail packaging; a weak category pick ("Cyberpunk-Like Games", "INSANE Combat") flops even on a working channel. Reused-trailer compilations are also a YouTube monetization-policy risk area — a taste reference for the *angle* (anticipation curation), not a production model to copy.
