# Content Engine & Production System — The No-Go Atlas

**Created:** 2026-07-19 · **Operator:** solo, faceless · **Cadence:** 6 videos/month planning
baseline inside the 1–2/week guardrail (DECISION.md M5) · **Budget:** 14–18 hr/video (M5) ·
**Launch:** early autumn 2026 so the first monetized month misses the Jan–Feb RPM trough (M7).

This document is the machine that turns the Access Ledger (`/production/access-ledger.md`) into
uploads without tripping the inauthentic-content pattern-match (M9, ypp-policy.md §2–4) and without
burning the ~100–130-place documented pool (skeptic-reports.md C14-policy §d).

## 1. The three episode formats (M3) — beat sheets

Rotation is a build requirement, not a preference: ≥2–3 structurally distinct episode types is the
Guardrail-6 differentiation answer AND the inauthentic-content answer in one (DECISION.md M9;
skeptic-reports.md C14-policy mod 2). Launch mix per launch-keyword-validation.md: 8 Deep Files,
2 Countdowns; Access-Calendar specials enter from video ~12.

Every format carries the four wedge devices (M1–M4): the **Entry File card**, the **myth-audit
beat**, the **"one legal way in" closer**, and the **documented-only filter**.

### Format A — Deep File (~20–30 min, 1 place/video)

The catalog workhorse (8 of 10 launch videos). Consumes 1 place from the pool — this is what makes
the pool last (skeptic-reports.md §d: countdowns burn ~10 places/video; deep files carry the tail).

| Beat | Time | Content |
|---|---|---|
| **Cold open** | 0:00–1:15 | In medias res on the *documented incident*: the prosecuted case, the revoked permit, the day the fence went up. Names, dates, one primary-source pull-quote on screen. No "what if I told you" filler — Engine A stakes with a citation under them (taste-signals T3/T8) |
| **Title card + premise** | 1:15–1:45 | Channel ident (stamp/dossier grammar). One-sentence promise: what the record actually shows, and the one legal way in at the end |
| **ENTRY FILE card** | 1:45–2:15 | Full-screen dossier rig (M1): ACCESS STATUS · LEGAL BASIS (named instrument, on screen) · PENALTY ON RECORD · LAST DOCUMENTED ENTRY. ~20–30 s, narrated. This is the visual identity and the author-input defense |
| **Ch. 1 — The Line on the Map** | 2:15–5:30 | Geography + map-zoom rig (Google Earth Studio, attribution intact). Where the boundary actually runs, who patrols it |
| **Ch. 2 — How It Closed** | 5:30–10:30 | The history: the event/decision that sealed it, told from dated documents. Archival assets subordinate to narration (ypp-policy §3.2) |
| **Ch. 3 — The Law** | 10:30–14:00 | The named instrument, quoted on screen with citation lower-third. What it actually prohibits vs what people assume it prohibits |
| **Ch. 4 — What the Record Shows Inside** | 14:00–19:00 | Documented interior: research reports, official photos, the last surveys. Only sourced material |
| **Ch. 5 — The Ones Who Tried** | 19:00–23:00 | Penalty on record + last documented entry, as narrative. Prosecutions, arrests, revoked permits — real cases only |
| **MYTH-AUDIT beat** | 23:00–25:30 | Grade the legend against the record (M4): state the famous claim, show what the documents support, issue a verdict (Confirmed / Distorted / Invented). Positions us *against* Atlas-Adam epistemics (taste-signals W3) |
| **"ONE LEGAL WAY IN" closer** | 25:30–27:30 | The documented aperture (M2): the permit, the protocol, the annual window, the replica — from the Access Ledger's aperture column. Rivals end at "you can't go"; we end at the door |
| **Next-episode hook** | 27:30–28:00 | One-line tease of the next slated place + end card (2 clickable slots). Tease with the *aperture*, not the prohibition ("next week: the island where the only way in is a job") |

### Format B — Countdown (~20–25 min, 8–10 places, place-reuse capped)

The discovery engine (launch #2 and #9; proof: 27K-sub channel, 169K views on a 21-min countdown —
launch-keyword-validation.md K9). Burns pool fast, so capped: no place reappears within 10 uploads;
max 2 countdown slots per place per rolling 12 months (Access Ledger maintenance rules).

| Beat | Time | Content |
|---|---|---|
| **Cold open** | 0:00–1:00 | The single hardest *documented* fact from the list (not the #1 reveal). Stakes + citation |
| **Premise + rules card** | 1:00–1:45 | The list's criterion, stated as a documentation standard: "every entry has a named law and a real penalty — and one legal way in" |
| **Entries N→2** (~2–2.5 min each) | 1:45–~19:00 | Per-entry mini-structure, identical rig, varied content: (1) rank card + map-zoom, (2) **60-second mini Entry File** (same dossier rig, condensed), (3) one document or dated case, (4) *either* a one-line myth flag *or* the aperture — alternate to vary rhythm |
| **MYTH-AUDIT beat** | mid-list | One full-length audit on the list's most mythologized entry (e.g., Area 51 map-blur claims) — the structural variation marker between countdowns |
| **Entry #1** | ~19:00–22:00 | Longest segment, full Entry File card, complete case |
| **"ONE LEGAL WAY IN" closer** | 22:00–24:00 | Rapid recap: the legal door into *each* entry, as a checklist screen — the packaging promise the title made ("...And the One Legal Way Into Each") |
| **Next-episode hook** | 24:00–24:30 | Tease the Deep File on one list entry ("Number 6 gets the full file next week") — countdown→deep-file funnel, and the reuse cap's one sanctioned echo |

### Format C — Access-Calendar / Status-Change Special (~15–25 min)

The format only we can run: the Access Ledger's status-change watch (rows with dated arcs: Varosha
2020, Okinoshima 2017, Uluru 2019, Hashima 2009, Gruinard 1990, Fukushima reopenings) feeds a
recurring "what changed and what it means" special. News-pegged when a change lands; otherwise an
annual-calendar edition ("every legal window into a forbidden place, month by month").

| Beat | Time | Content |
|---|---|---|
| **Cold open** | 0:00–1:00 | The moment of change: gates opening at Varosha, the last Okinoshima landing. Dated footage/reporting |
| **ENTRY FILE — BEFORE/AFTER card** | 1:00–1:45 | The dossier rig with a diff overlay: status, instrument, and aperture, before vs after — the format's signature variation of the M1 device |
| **Ch. 1 — The Paper That Changed It** | 1:45–6:00 | The instrument behind the change (resolution, ordinance, board decision), quoted |
| **Ch. 2 — What It Was / Ch. 3 — What It Is Now** | 6:00–14:00 | Compressed history + present state, per entry (specials can cover 1 place deep or 3–5 places briefly) |
| **MYTH-AUDIT beat** | 14:00–16:00 | The claims that circulated during the change vs the record |
| **"CALENDAR" closer** (format-specific M2 variant) | 16:00–19:00 | The forward-looking aperture: upcoming windows, pending reopenings, permit seasons — screenshot-able calendar card |
| **Next-episode hook** | 19:00–19:30 | Standard |

## 2. Weekly pipeline SOP — 1.5 videos/week solo, 14–18 hr/video

Per-video budget (M5, honoring the split): **research 4–5 h** (with citation log) · **script
4–5 h** · **VO 1–2 h** · **edit 5–6 h** · **package 1 h** ≈ 15–19 h nominal; template reuse
(section 3) is what pulls the realized number into the 14–18 band. Weekly load at 1.5
videos/week: **~22–27 h** — a heavy part-time job. That is the honest cost of 6/month.

The schedule runs a **2-week rolling cycle producing 3 videos**, with stages staggered so every
week touches three videos at different stages (research video C while editing video A). Batching
research is the key parallelization: one research day feeds multiple scripts because Access Ledger
rows share instrument families (e.g., both Andaman entries; all ZATO cities).

### The fortnight grid (videos A, B, C; upload slots: Tue + Sat wk1, Tue wk2 — pick and hold fixed slots)

| Day | Block (3–5 h) | Output |
|---|---|---|
| **W1 Mon** | Research A (4–5 h): primary-doc fetch + archive, citation log complete, Entry File facts locked | A: research done, Ledger row → Tier A |
| **W1 Tue** | Script A (4–5 h) from the citation log — no scripting from memory. *Upload slot: previous cycle's video ships today (already packaged)* | A: script v1 |
| **W1 Wed** | Script A polish + read-aloud pass (1 h) → VO A (1–2 h) → edit A starts: drop VO on template timeline, rough assembly (1–2 h) | A: VO done, rough cut started |
| **W1 Thu** | Edit A (4–5 h): Entry File card populated, map rig renders queued overnight, chapter builds | A: picture ~80% |
| **W1 Fri** | Edit A finish (1–2 h) + Package A (1 h): thumbnail (2 variants), title A/B candidates, description with citation list, "Altered content" check. Research B batch start (1–2 h) | A: DONE, scheduled. B: research started |
| **W1 Sat** | Research B finish (3 h). *Upload slot* | B: research done |
| **W1 Sun** | OFF (protected — burnout is a documented solo failure mode, taste-signals W6) | — |
| **W2 Mon** | Script B (4–5 h) | B: script v1 |
| **W2 Tue** | VO B (1–2 h) + edit B rough (2–3 h). *Upload slot* | B: assembly |
| **W2 Wed** | Edit B (4–5 h) | B: picture ~90% |
| **W2 Thu** | Finish + package B (2 h). Research C (2–3 h) | B: DONE. C: research started |
| **W2 Fri** | Research C finish (2 h) + script C (3 h) | C: script v1 |
| **W2 Sat** | Script C finish (1–2 h) → VO C (1–2 h) → edit C rough (1–2 h). *Upload slot* | C: assembly |
| **W2 Sun** | OFF | — |
| *(next W1 Mon–Tue)* | Edit + package C inside the next cycle's Mon–Tue while next-A research runs | C: DONE |

### Parallelization tricks

1. **Batch research by instrument family.** One session on the 1956 Andaman Regulation covers rows
   1 + 18; one ZATO session covers row 11 + all city segments; one UNSC session covers rows 4 + 32.
   Log once, cite many times.
2. **Template projects (section 3):** the editor opens a pre-built project with the Entry File rig,
   chapter cards, citation lower-thirds, music beds, and end card already on the timeline. Edit
   time is spent on content, not scaffolding — this is where 5–6 h/edit is won.
3. **Render queues overnight:** Google Earth Studio zooms and dossier-card renders queue at end of
   day, never watched.
4. **Countdown weeks are batch weeks:** one countdown = ten mini Entry Files; build all ten cards
   in one sitting from Ledger rows (the data is already structured for it).
5. **Package in pairs:** thumbnails and title A/Bs for two videos in one 2-h design sitting keeps
   the packaging eye consistent (packaging is THE variable per finalist-validation.md's HPD vs
   Untouched Mysteries spread).
6. **Buffer discipline:** the cycle above holds a ~1-video buffer at steady state. If the buffer
   hits zero twice in a month, drop to 5/month rather than break the 14–18 h floor — quality is
   load-bearing (M6 tripwire punishes weak packaging, and mass-production velocity is itself a
   policy signal, ypp-policy §4).

## 3. Production stack (faceless, policy-safe per M9)

### Scripts & research tooling

- **Citation log first, script second.** Per-video log (Obsidian/Notion database or a plain
  `citations/<place>.md`): claim → source → quote → URL/archive copy → fetch date. The Entry File
  card is generated from this log, never from memory. Archived primary docs live in
  `/production/citations/`.
- **Scripts human-authored in full.** AI may be used for outline critique or fact-list
  cross-checks, never for verbatim narration text (ypp-policy §3.1: "never narrate scraped or
  AI-dumped text"). **Keep all drafts** — script version history is provenance evidence for any
  YPP inauthentic-content review (ypp-policy §4).
- Read-aloud pass before VO: scripts are written for the ear (target ~130–150 wpm → a 26-min video
  is ~3,600–3,900 words).

### Voiceover — the decision, with tradeoffs (M9: human-directed narration with named persona)

The channel builds a **named narrator persona** from video 1 regardless of which voice option runs —
a consistent authorship signal is an explicit YPP review signal (ypp-policy §3.5, §4).

| Option | Pros | Cons | Disclosure duty |
|---|---|---|---|
| **A. Own voice (recommended default)** | Zero marginal cost; strongest possible authenticity/authorship evidence; no disclosure question; direction is free (re-takes on the spot) | Requires a serviceable read (pacing/energy — practice cost); ties the channel to one human's availability; accent/consistency risk on off days | None |
| **B. Premium TTS, human-directed, named persona** | Consistency; speed (1–2 h VO block becomes ~45 min); re-takes are free; survives illness/travel | Recurring cost; "robotic narration reading text the creator didn't write" is a named demonetization trigger — mitigated ONLY by original human scripts + audible direction (pacing marks, emphasis passes, pronunciation control); classifier risk in a lane YouTube already sees polluted (skeptic-reports.md C14-policy §b) | **If the synthetic voice is realistic and presented as a real person, check "Altered content" at upload** (ypp-policy §2: disclosure is monetization-safe; concealment is not). State the persona honestly in the About page |
| Hybrid (own voice launch → TTS backup) | De-risks review period: the channel's first-impression corpus is human | Voice change mid-catalog is noticeable | As above when TTS used |

Decision: **launch on own voice if a listenable read is achievable in 2 practice weeks; else Option
B with the full direction + disclosure protocol.** Never flat undirected TTS — that is the canonical
rejection example (ypp-policy §3.8).

### Editing — template-driven

- **NLE:** DaVinci Resolve (free tier sufficient; Studio if noise reduction needed). One master
  **template project** per episode format (3 templates total — the per-format structural
  differences are themselves the M9 variation evidence).
- Template contents: intro/ident, Entry File rig slot, chapter card generators, citation
  lower-third preset, color grade, music bed structure, end card. Per-video work = populate, don't
  build.
- **Per-video variation duty (M9):** vary chapter order where the story allows, vary map/asset mix,
  vary the myth-audit placement — the template is a rig library, not a fixed sequence. Reviewers
  must see per-video structural variation (ypp-policy §3.4).

### Maps & motion — the two reusable rigs (the visual identity)

1. **The Entry File dossier card** (M1): a motion-graphics rig (Resolve Fusion or After Effects)
   styled as a stamped case file — four fields typed on, stamp hit on ACCESS STATUS, citation
   footer. Built once, populated per segment from the Ledger row. This card is simultaneously the
   brand, the citation floor made visible, and the author-input defense (DECISION.md M1).
2. **The map-zoom rig**: Google Earth Studio orbit/zoom templates (attribution rules in section 4)
   plus a QGIS/NaturalEarth custom-map style for boundary overlays the satellite view can't show
   (exclusion perimeters, buffer zones). Self-made map animation is the "visual spine, not
   garnish" requirement (skeptic-reports.md C14-policy §b: slideshow-adjacency is C14's structural
   weak point — the rigs are the countermeasure).

### Music / SFX licensing

- One subscription library (Epidemic Sound / Artlist class) covering YouTube monetization, receipts
  retained; or YouTube Audio Library (free, attribution rules per track). No "royalty-free" rips
  from other videos, no trending-audio grabs.
- SFX (stamp hits, page turns, radio static) from the same subscription or CC0 archives
  (freesound with CC0 filter); keep a per-video asset manifest (section 4's discipline applies to
  audio too).

## 4. Asset sourcing table — licensing discipline

Rule zero (Guardrail 6 / reused-content safe harbor, ypp-policy §2): **every third-party asset is
subordinate to original narration/analysis — no segment is footage-led.** Every video ships with an
asset manifest (source, license, proof link) stored alongside the citation log.

| Source class | Examples | License/terms | Usage rules for us | Verify before launch |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **US public-domain archives** | Library of Congress (loc.gov), NARA, Prelinger/Internet Archive newsreels | US federal government works: public domain. LOC/NARA host some third-party-rights items | Check per-item rights statement — "no known restrictions" ≠ blanket PD. Credit source anyway (trust signal). Newsreels only inside an analytical frame, never as filler montage | Spot-check rights statements per item at download time |
| **Wikimedia Commons** | Place photos, historical images | Mixed: PD, CC0, CC-BY, CC-BY-SA | Filter to license per file; **attribution exactly as the license requires** (author + license, on-screen credit or description block); CC-BY-SA does not force video share-alike for mere inclusion but attribute scrupulously; avoid NC-licensed files entirely (we monetize) | Per-file license capture in asset manifest |
| **Government / UN footage** | UN Web TV, UNIFEED, national agency footage, NASA | NOT uniformly free. US federal = PD; UN audiovisual has its own usage guidelines (news/educational use, no implied UN endorsement); other governments assert crown/state copyright | Treat each national source individually; UN materials per their published AV-library terms — fetch and archive the terms page before first use; NASA imagery free with credit | **UN AV-library terms; any crown-copyright source** |
| **News footage (fair use)** | Short excerpts of broadcast coverage of documented incidents | Fair use is a defense, not a license | **Commentary-transformation duty (Guardrail 6):** short excerpt, directly analyzed by narration, meaning added, never the segment's spine; the video must be pointless without our layer (ypp-policy §3.2). Prefer paraphrase + still + citation over clip where possible. Expect and budget for occasional Content ID claims on excerpts | Keep per-use fair-use rationale line in the asset manifest |
| **Google Earth Studio / Google Earth** | Map zooms, orbits (the map rig) | Per Google's Geo guidelines + Earth Studio docs (Tier B-verified this session, 2026-07-19): monetized YouTube use for educational/entertainment purposes is permitted; **attribution to Google Earth + imagery providers required and is auto-watermarked per frame — never crop or cover it**; NO use in ads/promotional content | Keep watermark visible; add verbal/description credit; do not use frames in the *thumbnail* if that reads as promotional | Re-verify terms from an unblocked network pre-launch (session caveat, ypp-policy sourcing note) |
| **Open satellite imagery** | Copernicus/Sentinel, Landsat (USGS) | Free with attribution (Copernicus credit line; USGS/NASA credit) | Good for exclusion-zone before/after (Varosha, Fukushima, Wittenoom); attribution line on screen | Standard credit strings |
| **Commercial satellite** | Maxar, Planet press imagery | Licensed; press releases ≠ creator license | **Avoid** unless via a properly licensed news-agency route; do not lift from news articles | — |
| **AI-generated imagery** | Stylized reconstructions, texture/background plates | Allowed by YouTube; disclosure rules apply | Stylized/clearly-non-photoreal only for historical reconstruction; **NEVER photoreal AI depictions of real places/events presented as real** — that mandates "Altered content" disclosure and, undisclosed, risks strikes (ypp-policy §2). House style: obviously illustrated. Disclose whenever realistic synthetic media is used; don't disclose-spam on clearly stylized art | Log AI-asset usage per video in the manifest for the upload checklist |
| **Own assets** | Entry File rig, QGIS maps, charts, timelines | Ours | The visual spine — target ≥50% of on-screen time from own/rig assets in every video (the anti-slideshow metric) | — |

## 6. Analytics tripwire protocol (M6)

Checkpoints at **uploads 4, 8, and 12** (roughly weeks 3, 6, 9 at 1.5/wk). Metrics pulled per video
at 7 and 28 days, plus channel run-rate:

| Metric | Healthy signal | Reference |
|---|---|---|
| CTR (browse impressions) | ≥4% early; watch trend, not absolutes | packaging is THE variable (finalist-validation.md HPD 62.9K mean vs Untouched Mysteries 2.1K) |
| Avg % viewed on 20–30 min videos | ≥35–40% | watch-hours are the YPP gate; long-form AVD drives it |
| Views at 28 days | best video trending toward 5 figures | tripwire below |
| Channel run-rate (views/mo) | ≥30K by upload 8–10 | skeptic-reports.md C14-revenue mod 3 |
| Views/sub conversion | ~100–140 views/sub band | niche comps: Ancient Indy ~135.8, Atlas Adam ~105.8 (skeptic-reports.md C14-revenue §e) |
| Browse vs search mix | search-led early, browse growing | discovery-driven thesis (taste-signals T4) |

**Checkpoint 4:** no action thresholds — collect baselines; kill only obviously broken thumbnails
(sub-2% CTR with impressions) via swap, not overhaul.

**Checkpoint 8 — THE TRIPWIRE (M6):** if after 8–10 uploads **no video has exceeded 10K views** AND
run-rate is **<30K views/mo** → the packaging thesis is failing on the exact observable that
separates HPD from Untouched Mysteries. Trigger the overhaul, not month-6 hope.

**Packaging-overhaul playbook** (2-week sprint, uploads pause at 1/wk):
1. Re-run the title A/B stock: 20 fresh titles that are simultaneously (a) not Ancient Indy's
   prohibition skeleton, (b) citation-anchored, (c) hard-hook per W7 (the skeptic's pre-launch list
   refreshed against live CTR data — skeptic-reports.md C14-revenue mod 4).
2. Thumbnail system rebuild: test dossier/stamp/permit grammar vs place-forward photography on the
   3 best-performing topics (re-package existing uploads first — free experiments).
3. Cold-open surgery on the 3 worst retention curves (first-60-seconds drop-off is packaging too).
4. If differentiated titles systematically underperform the cloned formula by W7 magnitude (6–8x),
   the channel premise is contradicted by its own data — escalate to re-judgment immediately, per
   the skeptic's mod 4; do not wait for month 6.

**Checkpoint 12 / kill criteria:** if after the overhaul plus 4 more uploads (≈ uploads 12–14)
there is STILL no >10K video and run-rate remains <30K/mo, the channel is drawing the
Untouched-Mysteries outcome (~$60–160/mo indefinitely, finalist-validation.md) → **kill or pivot**
per M6. State it in the log and stop sinking the 22–27 h weeks. Additional standing kill checks:
any Community Guidelines strike during ramp (YPP-blocking, ypp-policy §1) and the M8 RPM
re-verification failing badly (creator-reported RPM ≤$3 → view target becomes ≥667K/mo,
skeptic-reports.md C14-revenue mod 2 — re-judge economics before month 6).
